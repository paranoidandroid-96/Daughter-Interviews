# sweethearts dressed in black
words kelly griffin, portrait philippa johnson

## daughter are a band who write in the dark

**All clad in dark clothes, the members of Daughter make a striking patch of darkness against the walls of the whitewashed photography studio we're in. It's rather fitting considering the band's bone-chilling dolorous lyrics about break-ups and breakdowns. "Setting fire to our insides for fun / collecting names of the lovers that went wrong", runs a typical line, from the song Youth.**

**Elena Tonra's vocals grace their debut album, If You Leave, accompanied by her parter Igor Haefeli on guitar and percussionist Remi Aguilella. But, let's start from the beginning.**

**Elena, you had been performing solo before Daughter, but the band really began with you and Igor playing together. Can you remember when you and Igor first met?**

**Elena:** We were on the same songwriting course. It was our very first day and they shoved us all into one room and said we had to play one of our compositions. I was really nervous. I'm not the most natural performer. I didn't bring anything with me because I wasn't expecting to play on the first day, so I had to borrow a guitar from someone, and I borrowed a capo from Igor, which (she turns to Igor) was very nice of you, thank you. We were in the same class for three terms and I can just remember Igor making some pretty badass music.

**Last year, the three of you hired an old converted chapel where you wrote the songs that would make up your debut album. Do you write together, or do the songs start with Elena writing alone?**

**Igor:** The process is different for each song, but generally it needs to come from Elena because the lyrics need to be genuine for the music to be truthful.

**Elena, do you have a particular space where you write best?**

**Elena:** Usually, it's a guitar, a dark room and maybe a drink, and I just see what happens. The lyrics are definitely personal, or at least truthful. They don't have to be a word-for-word account of something, but they need to come from somewhere that is in me. I don't think about it too much. The lyrics write themselves; it falls out of the brain and onto the notebook.

**Some of the lyrics are quite dark and almost painfully honest, which is brave.**

**Igor:** I think there have been times when she's come up with a song and...

**Elena:** ... I sort of forget that I've said something.

**Igor:** And when she realises what she's saying, then maybe she almost chickens out.

**So that's where the rest of the band come in?**

**Igor:** Yes and, Elena, you have said many times that what helps is to have people around you that accompany you in your songs and it's not just you alone on stage.

**Elena:** Sometimes I do forget, and some of the songs have pretty weird lyrics. Smother has some pretty weird lyrics at the end ("sometimes I wish I stayed inside / my mother / never to come out") and I just don't think about it. It means a lot to me, that song, but someone was like, "Umm, you say this at the end...?" and I didn't know what to say.

**Elena, are your parents musical, or do you come from a creative family?**

**Elena:** No, not really. My parents love music. They're massive music lovers, and they were very much into Neil Young and Bob Dylan and lots of the great songwriters. My grandfather was great at singing; he was amazing, actually.

**Did he ever teach you any music growing up?**

**Elena:** No. He's Irish and used to sing traditional Irish folk songs. I think he may have had the opportunity whe he was younger to pursue a musical career, because someone made him an offer. He turned it down and took another job because it offered more money and he had a massive family to look after, six kids. So he didn't pursue it in the end, which is really sad. I'm happy that I am a musician. I'm like, "Grandad, I'm doing it!"

**Igor, you joked before, or perhaps you were serious, that when you're on tour you request tea in your rider. Is that true?**

**Igor:** We *do* request tea in our rider. What I want to do is have a rider for each venue sent out in advance and ask for different types of tea in each, so that at the end we have a massive collection of fifteen sorts of teas. At the moment we generally get English Breakfast.

**Elena:** Which is fine for me!

**The band toured quite a lot last year, and you even performed on the Late Show with David Letterman, which is no small feat considering you didn't even have an album out at that stage. With the album out at the end of March, this could be a pretty big year for the three of you.**

**Remi:** We'll see. Let's talk about it after the album comes out.

**Igor:** Last year was very much about making the album, even when we were on tour, so this year, we can just enjoy it.

**Elena:** I'm itching to write more. I can just feel myself, (she scratches at her black jeans) I'll need to...

**Find a dark room?**

**Elena:** Yes, find somewhere dark to stark writing and drinking again.
