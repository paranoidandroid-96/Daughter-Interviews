# Down to the bones

## *As Ex:Re, Elena Tonra used a break from her band Daughter to explore the aftermath of a relationship - a painful but necessary exercise in catharsis*

words holly williams\
portrait liz seabrook 

**You could be forgiven for thinking Ex:Re was a classic break-up album. Written while heartbroken by Elena Tonra, lead singer of Daughter (here also recording as Ex:Re), the name is a twist on “regarding ex”. And the record’s winding, melancholic songs feature lyrics such as “I see your fingerprints on household things/I'm just too sad to touch”, and the bald “I miss him and I hate it”.**

But that isn't quite the full story. “It's pronounced X-ray and it's a play with that - because it is regarding that person, but it's also seeing into the relationship and the reality of it. Sometimes, when you look back, maybe it wasn't all wonderful,” says Tonra when we met in the cosy recording studio in the basement of her label, 4AD.

“I think actually it's more a record about myself, healing, than it is about something ending. It's regarding him, but he's not really in it. Which is just funny: I've basically made a break-up album about... me.” She laughs, and adds “How telling!”

Tonra jokes a lot during our interview, a twinkle in her big pale eyes as she adopts funny little voices when it gets too doom-and-gloomy or when effusing over her new collaborators Fabian Prynn and cellist Josephine Stephenson. But she's also extremely honest about how tough the past couple of years have been; when reading back over the transcript, the pain reeks off it - as it does with the album. 

And this is the edited version. “I didn't want to make an album like, 'Please don't leave me', and a lot of the initial writing was 'Oh God, this is the worst thing ever!' I like the ones about the time since - maybe a bit 'I miss you', which is true, but more exploring my behaviour. That interested me more than pining after someone.”

The record was written in a splurge, Tonra “vomiting” up lengthy poems, notes, and letters to herself, desperately trying to make sense of her feelings; some of it was discarded, but some became her lyrics.

Tonra’s period of self-exploration will be wincingly familiar to anyone who's had a serious life-crisis kind of break-up. Lyrics describe seeing your ex everywhere you turn, the burn of watching coupled-up mates, but also the exhilaration of meeting new people, of working out who you are when alone and the hazy allure of getting smashed (“wine-wasted, shit-faced, solo, so what?” she exhales on ‘New York’).

That was another reason for the name, some distance from this version of herself.

> *“It's more a record about myself, healing, than it is about something ending”*

“Well, I do get drunk very often, and I am kind of down,” she laughs, “but it's not really, you know, me...” Because Tonra is moving on, has found some "peace". Writin has been therapeutic, but this will be likely the only Ex:Re record: “It's a nice way to let it go. And hopefully not in any way that would negatively affect the other person - I just wanted to do this because I couldn't get over it. It helped; it's lessened how sad the whole thing was for me.”

What about Daughter, then? The band which Tonra formed with Igor Haefeli and Remi Aguilella in 2011, was on a six-month break anyway, “because we'd been on a loop of writing, recording and touring, for seven years. We were all supposed to have a holiday, but this turned into my holiday...”

Daughter records have always featured her lyrics - and sonically Ex:Re's moody atmospheric, ambiently layered indie-rock isn't a million miles away - but this album had to be a  solo effort. Recording with the band is a collaborative process, each song a shifting, negotiable thing; as Ex:Re, it was her vision "coloured in" by Prynn and Stephenson. “Igor will be like, 'Where's the chorus?' Maybe I was a bit indulgent having all the lyrics in there, six-minute songs...”

Ex:Re was recorded very fast, using live sessions overdubbed in the studio, and released digitally in November within a few weeks; a physical release followed at the start of February. This reflected the urgency of Tonra's writing - “I needed to just get it out” - but also the fun she had recording. She speaks warmly of the energy Stephenson and Prynn brought “which thsi record desperately called for! It was so lonely for a very long time.”

Now, she's ready to return to her band. Daughter have had some writing sessions and Tonra is feeling positive. “I'd like to think this album closes the book on that place, that feeling, so I can have a new start - writing about something that isn't my post-break-up days.”

*Ex:Re* is out now on 4AD
