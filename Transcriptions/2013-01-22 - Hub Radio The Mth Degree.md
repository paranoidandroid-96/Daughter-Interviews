# 2013-01-22 - Hub Radio The Mth Degree

*It's the Mth Degree, on Hub Radio.*

**Daniel:** The snow is certainly snowing here in Bristol tonight, but we're tucked away in the small, eerie, resonant area of St. George's, Bristol, with some very warm-hearted guests indeed. Guys, would you like to introduce yourselves?

**Igor:** Hello, my name's Igor.

**Elena:** I'm Elena.

**Remi:** And I'm Remi.

**Igor:** And we're Daughter.

[Youth]

**Daniel:** With just a few dates left on this mammoth tour, you zipped all the way down from Glasgow last night. So, credit to you there. It's sold out here tonight at St. George's. You've had similar venues on your tour that have been like this. How does it make you feel standing up in front of a pack that cried?

**Remi:** It's great, it's a great honour to play in front of a lot of people. And knowing that they... It's crazy for me to think that they just come to see us, in a way. I mean, we've played bigger crowds at festivals, but it's always like, you know, people come there and kind of wander around during the festival, but, you know, as your own show, it's crazy.

**Elena:** And given the weather conditions as well, I think it's really... It's going to be really great to actually see people, because I think a lot of people are worried about the snow and stuff really screwing with their journey, so it'll be really lovely.

[Youth]

**Daniel:** Coming on to venues, actually, you said you've been playing, you know, you've been playing bigger venues, obviously you supported the two Bens, as we like to call them, Ben Leftwich and Ben Howard, around arenas. But coming into these really sort of close-quarters venues, like Union Chapel and here in Bristol and St George's, where people are sat down in front of you, and it's all very nice and cosy and comfy in front of you, I mean, which do you prefer?

**Igor:** We enjoy pretty much everything, really. We've definitely played a lot of seated venues this tour, and, you know, when we had one show that wasn't seated, and that was really great as well. I think the one thing that we fear with the seated venues is that it's very quiet, and that adds pressure on to us, because we feel kind of almost like analysed and, you know, under scrutiny. At the same time, it's really nice to know that people are really listening to music, and, you know, for example, when we're playing churches, you know, the acoustics are so amazing. But then, you know, we kind of like also the rowdiness of, like, you know, dive bars and stuff like that.

[Youth]

**Daniel:** Elena, I was just going to come on to you as we're coming on to the music a bit now. So, coming on to the music, obviously, you started out first, and then everybody sort of came together as a sort of a great triangle. I like three people in a band. I have this thing about triangles, I don't know why. Yeah, so just coming on to the music and the lyrics, I read somewhere one report said that you held nothing back with your songs, and they felt watching you, they felt like the lyrics were laid bare to the audience. I mean, how, did you get used to sharing something so personal as songwriting? I mean, songwriting is a very personal thing. Do you ever get used to standing in front of the audience and basically exposing yourself, I guess?

**Elena:** You make me sound like a flasher. No, I think it's, I think it's, when you write a song, it almost, well, for me anyway, kind of it definitely takes a different form to a memory. I think it's almost a kind of a completely different, a completely different thing in a way. So once it's in song form, for some reason it's a little bit more manageable, and I think it's easier to kind of go out and sing it and not, maybe not always think about why you wrote it. And then there are other times where, you know, where you stand there and then when you think about it, because you more think about the music when it's made into a song, and you think about the music and the textures and all the sort of things that we do around it. So it's when you actually stand there and really think about why you wrote it, that's when it gets difficult. And I think that's when things have happened a few times.

[Youth]

[Landfill]

**Daniel:** I've had someone ask, if I could ask this question to you, they're not here tonight, but they've asked me, they're one of the listeners of our show, and it's from Beth Davis in Newport, and she said she's a songwriter, and your songs, like, influence her music so much. She's just wondering if you could just give her a few words of inspiration for her next track that she's about to write.

**Elena:** I don't know. 

**Daniel:** Think about it. You could be making a number one here.

**Elena:** I don't know if I'm the best person at all for this.

**Daniel:** Three words.

**Elena:** Just be truthful.

[Candles]

**Daniel:** Okay, so coming onto the album, the album, the album, the album. A lot of people, I mean, you've got two EP's out at the moment. Everybody's been talking about the album. It's only just recently been announced. Within the past week, I read it on the NME website. For those people who have not encountered this story, could you guys just share the announcement with us?

**Igor:** Our album is coming out in March.

**Elena:** The 18th?

**Igor:** I think it's the 18th, maybe.

**Elena:** You're rubbish.

**Igor:** It's coming out on 4AD, and there'll be 10 tracks on it, quite a few new ones. 

**Daniel:** How do you think it will compare to the previous EP's that have been released, this new album?

**Igor:** It's definitely darker and heavier in certain ways. I think there's, I think we just kind of, you know, how do you say that in English? Sorry. Dotted our I's.

**Daniel:** Oh, crossed the T's.

**Igor:** Yeah, crossed the T's, dotted the I's. Definitely experimented some more as well. Tried lots of different things, some of which is on the album, some of which is not.

[Candles]

**Daniel:** We're sat in January now. I'm just wondering if you guys made any new resolutions, and if you did, have you kept them? Remi?

**Remi:** Well, I kind of thought I was going to be starting to exercise again, but I'll just say that I haven't started. So technically, I haven't failed as well.

**Elena:** I'm not sure I like to be. Maybe no.

**Remi:** No? Okay, well then Elena, okay. Then I definitely failed from the first day.

**Elena:** I was given my new year's resolution by somebody else that happens to be our manager, to actually reply to emails and talk to people on the phone as well. Because I'm not very good at communicating, so that was a good one. So I've tried to keep to it. Hopefully. Everyone's like, that's my reply.

**Daniel:** Igor?

**Igor:** I don't really, didn't make any new resolutions. I constantly have some, but I don't like, haven't set myself one or two. I just think I should do this this year, kind of thing.

**Daniel:** When can we expect you back, guys?

**Igor:** Maybe at a festival around during the summer. Maybe, I don't know yet. And then, yeah, we'll probably come back, I think, towards autumn. 

**Daniel:** Fantastic. Well, Remi, Elena and Igor, it's been absolutely amazing chatting to you here tonight at St George's. Thank you. It's going to be a sold out crowd. I'm really excited. Thank you very much and good luck.

**Igor:** Thank you! 

**Daniel:** Cheers.
