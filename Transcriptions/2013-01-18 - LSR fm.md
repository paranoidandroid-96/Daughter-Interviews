# 2013-01-18 - LSR fm

**Isabelle:** My name's Izzy, you're listening to Leeds Student Radio, and I'm here with Elena and Remi from Daughter. Say hi.

**Elena:** Hello.

**Remi:** Hi.

**Isabelle:** Am I pronouncing your names right?

**Elena:** Mine's actually Elena, but it's fine, it's spelt funny.

**Isabelle:** I do apologise, Elena. I'll remember that from now. Okay, so how are you guys? You're on your Headline UK tour. How's it going?

**Elena:** It's going well. Actually, this is our second show. Well, this is our second night of the tour. And yes, it's going well. Yesterday went well, we were in Norwich, and hopefully tonight will be good.

**Isabelle:** I first discovered you guys when you supported Benjamin Francis Leftwich on his tour about a year and a half ago. So I was just wondering, how did it get from you doing little acoustic gigs supporting him to signing to 4AD Records and having your own tours and that kind of thing?

**Elena:** Yeah, I'm not quite sure really how that happened. It's kind of an amazing step, really. I think we, yeah, I don't know, we just sort of, we're gigging quite a lot. And then we had our own headline show last Christmas time, which was at St. Giles, which is a church in London. And basically, yeah, Ed from 4AD came down and really liked us. So it's kind of, it sort of happened, yeah, quite gradually. It really wasn't, you know, we didn't like rush into things. We were sort of, we took our time and we definitely played live a lot. You know, we did a lot of shows and wanted to make our live shows something special. So, yeah, it was a gradual process, but it's still, you know, it's still surprising that it happened. So, yeah. 

**Isabelle:** Yeah, I saw your face when I said that you'd signed. You still look sort of a little bit shocked by that.

**Remi:** I still can't really believe it. You know, it's a bit crazy. You like, I personally grew up listening to a band like the Pixies or Cocteau Twins. It's just suddenly a label gets in contact with you and they say they want to sign you. It's just amazing.

**Isabelle:** It's just been announced your release date for your album is March 18th. So tell me a little bit about that.

**Elena:** Oh, well, it's kind of different. I mean, it's not like we haven't gone reggae or anything, but it's quite dark. I think it will be, you know, it's definitely not party music. I think if you put it on, you'd kill every vibe in the room. But no, I think it's, I don't know. It was we've got quite a lot of new songs on there. We've only actually got two older songs that we reworked from from sort of the past and then one song from an EP and that was kind of it. So it's all very new material, which I think is really exciting. I think we really wanted to progress somewhere with this and not just make the same stuff again. But it's also quite worrying because no one's really ever heard it before. So we're like, "oh God, please like it". But yeah, I don't know. It's, it'll be interesting.
I've kind of, I can't listen to it because we sort of finished it now. And I just need, I just... I went... I went a bit insane when we were recording. It was like "Argh!", so yeah, I think it's better to like just let it go. And and yeah, yeah, we'll see.

**Isabelle:** Will we maybe get to hear a few preview tracks in the set tonight?

**Elena:** Yes. Yeah, there'll be two, three, two. No. Yeah, two. Well, I say two. There's two unheard ones that we're going to play Smother and then we're going to play two called Tomorrow and In the Shallows as well, which are ones that we have actually played live before. So I think if anyone has seen us live before, they would have heard them. So there's only two like "Surprise!" ones.

**Isabelle:** Yeah, I'll keep an eye out for those two. OK, so talk to me about your music because it's very dark, as you say, and a bit of a mood killer if you're playing at a party. Do you ever feel like a bit sort of exposed or vulnerable, like performing that kind of dark personal material to an audience full of people?

**Elena:** Yeah, it is pretty exposing. I think it's difficult when you sort of when you really concentrate on the fact that it's about something, I think if you just think of it as this is a song, you can kind of get through the set. And that's kind of how I, you know, lyrics wise, how I deal with seeing stuff that's so weird. But I think if you think about it, I mean, it is about like I have actually cried on stage, which is really embarrassing. And I try not to because it's never a good look. But yeah, I think you have to really distance yourself from what it's about to be able to perform it. And I think it's always the thoughts that were there when you wrote it kind of dissolve a little bit when you write a song because it's, you know, it's a bit more it's more poetic or whatever, you know, it's slightly more distilled into I guess.

**Isabelle:** So the reason why people connect with your music and the reason why people want to come and see you play is because they could because it's so emotional and because it's so sort of real. So in a way, I don't think you do look stupid crying on stage. I wouldn't think so anyway. And like, let's face it, Adele did it at the Brits.

**Elena:** If I cry this evening, I'll look at you and be like "Gaaah!" 

**Isabelle:** I'll give you a thumbs up. Okay, so cracking on, I'm going to be self-indulgent now. My favorite one of your tracks is Run, which I believe was the B-side to Smother. And that's my favorite track, I think, because it just has a very sort of good sense of narrative. Like it really tells a story. So I was wondering if you could tell me about that track.

**Elena:** I don't really know what to say. It is a very story-like. I think that, you know, it's probably the most, not fictional, but the most sort of, how can I say, events that are recorded in order kind of thing. But I mean, obviously it's more based on an emotion than actual events, which I think is quite an interesting thing to write because it was based on something that really the song would seem like. It's about something else. I don't know if that makes any sense.

**Isabelle:** Is it quite an old song? Because I found it a little while ago on YouTube, just sort of like live versions that you'd done and was a little bit obsessive in trying to find like a sort of proper studio track. And then when I saw that it was the B-side, I was like, yes!

**Elena:** Yeah, it's pretty old. It's probably a couple of years old now, actually.

**Isabelle:** Well, I'm really happy that you put it on there. So when I was researching this interview, I found a Guardian New Band of the Day article, which I'm assuming you've read before. And in that, it has quite an interesting quote at the bottom. They always do most likely to and least likely to. And they said, most likely to stretch out on your grave, least likely to go out raving. How accurate would you say that was?

**Elena:** Yeah, quite accurate. 

**Igor:** Although we would go raving.

**Elena:** We probably might go raving, but yeah, probably not in the same way, most people go raving.

**Isabelle:** How does Daughter go raving?

**Elena:** Subtly, I think. We'd sort of just stand there a bit like, oh, I feel slightly uncomfortable, but I'm here and I'm out and everyone's here.

**Remi:** There are a lot of people.

**Igor:** It just depends how much we had to drink.

**Elena:** Drink is the deciding factor. I think if we're right over the edge with the drinking, we'll be right in there with the raving. I think it's like a drink to rave ratio.

**Isabelle:** Fantastic. Will you be out raving in Leeds tonight, do you think, after the gig?

**Elena:** Absolutely, yeah, definitely. I think I will. 

**Isabelle:** And what about stretching out on people's graves? Is that something you do often?

**Elena:** Yeah, every weekend. Yeah, I don't know. He said some funny things in that. I wasn't sure if I was totally enamored with everything that guy said. That is pretty funny, so I have to commend him on that one. 

**Isabelle:** Do you tend to read a lot of your own criticism?

**Elena:** No, I really try not to. I think we were sent that one. Like, this is the first thing anyone's ever wrote about you. Since then, we were like, "Right, let's not read these anymore".

**Isabelle:** What is the most ridiculous thing you've ever read that a critic has written about you?

**Elena:** I don't know, actually. I'm not sure. 

**Remi:** It's not crazy, but there's someone said once that we're considered as f'd up folk. And I kind of like that. It's not crazy. It's not but kind of interesting. Interesting way of seeing it because I have no idea how to describe my music ever. So I just...

**Isabelle:** Okay. Well, I think we'll round it off there. Thank you very much. It's been nice to meet you all and look forward to seeing your gig tonight at Holy Trinity.
